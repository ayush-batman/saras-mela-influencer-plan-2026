Creative guardrails

Candid, not posed. Let artisans speak in their own words. No unverified personal income claims or stat-dumping. Disclose paid partnerships clearly, tag the brand handle and agree raw-footage/reshares in the contract.

Creative strategy

Meet the makers. Stay for the mela.

The women who make the mela are the reason to come. Open on a maker and her work, then invite the city as a host, not a promoter: “She’s here till the 22nd. Come say hello.” Food, craft and evening energy keep the stories alive; the maker stays the subject and the visit stays the ask.

The content system

Ten phone-shot reels, two original stories per creator, available light and consent before filming. Suggested reel spread: three Makers’ Table, three Her Hands, two Guess the Price, two One Day, One Woman. Story one discovers a person, stall or craft; story two gives the reason and the way to visit. Assign formats to creator strengths after a portfolio check.

01Process · 30-45s

Her Hands

Start with sound and an extreme close-up of hands at work. Pull back to the maker; ask one question and let her answer in her own words, subtitled where needed. Show the finished piece and verified stall number. Close: “She’s at stall __, Punnami Ghat, till Oct 22.” Best for textile, home and wearable-craft voices.

02Discovery · 20-30s

Guess the Price

Hold up an object: “Guess how much this is.” After a quick guess, let the maker state the price and describe the work behind it. The reveal honours time and skill, never a bargain. End with the verified stall and a reason to see it in person. Best for local discovery and lifestyle voices.

03Food trail · 30-45s

The Makers’ Table

Open on steam and an honest first-person food reaction. Meet the woman running the stall; hear where the dish comes from in her words. Show the dish, stall, timing and checked price. Close with a warm family invitation. Give this accessible visit hook to the strongest Vijayawada food voices.

04Follow film · 45-60s

One Day, One Woman

Open before the crowds as a woman sets up. Follow her work, a brief conversation, fellow makers with permission, the ground around her and a natural shared pause if it happens. Close at dusk with the invitation. The handloom creator’s flagship and one food-stall maker story; at most two follow films, so each feels personal.

Hook bank for creators to adapt.

English lines are starting points, not scripts. Telugu phrasing is for creators to write naturally. Confirm every named fact on the ground.

  1. “Her hands know something a machine doesn’t.”
  2. “Guess how much this costs. Then hear what went into it.”
  3. “She set this stall up before the city woke up.”
  4. “I’ve eaten all over Vijayawada. I’ve never had this.”
  5. “Ask her how many days this sari took. She’s at stall __.” Only if she gives the number.
  6. “The women who make this mela are the reason to come.”
  7. “Not a sale. Not the exhibition you remember. This is different.”
  8. “Punnami Ghat, before October 22. Randi, chudandi.”
  1. “Your home is missing something. She made it.”
  2. “The stalls here are run by women. Ask them anything.”
  3. “If you buy one thing this month, buy it from her hands.”
  4. “This is what [her region] tastes like. In Vijayawada. This week only.” Name the region only if she confirms it.
  5. “Last weekend of the mela. She packs up on the 22nd.”
  6. “Bring your mother. She’ll meet women who speak her language.”
  7. “Skip the mall this weekend. Mela undi Punnami Ghat lo.”

Process hooks 1, 5, 9 and 11 fit Her Hands; 2 fits Guess the Price; 4 and 12 fit Makers’ Table; 3 fits the follow film. Save the final-weekend lines 13-15 for the closing wave.

Never do

No posed “I’m at the mela” openings, generic venue tours, invented backstories, discount-bait or government-script voiceovers. No personal income claim or Lakhpati Didi reference without the maker’s and organizer’s on-record confirmation. No staged candid moment, unconsented visitor or child as content. Verify any named dish, stall or performance on the day. Disclose paid partnerships on every asset and follow contracted tagging terms.

05 / Method

Fit before follower count.

Selection and pricing are hypotheses to test in outreach, not confirmed creator commitments.

  1. Category fitFood voices cover stalls and rural produce; Bhuvi and Divya cover handloom; Neha and Jaya follow handicraft, textiles and home-use rural products.
  2. Story fitPrioritize creators whose existing format supports candid visits, maker conversations and material close-ups.
  3. Geographic fit — Vijayawada locals for city relevance; Andhra and Telangana for regional discovery; the Kochi home voice is conditional on travel quotes. Confirm all travel origins and quotes before booking.
  4. Audience check — third-party follower counts are only a first pass. Ask for native audience-city shares, the median of recent branded-reel views, retention and story completion, and check format fit.
06 / Measurement

Measure what moved.

Awareness and intent are different jobs. Report both, and keep footfall claims honest.

100-200K · two creators

Reach, reel views, engagement rate and story completion for campaign awareness.

50-100K + 10-50K · eight creators

Reel saves and shares, comment quality, story navigation and sticker/link taps when available.

All ten

Brand tags, reshares and contracted raw-footage handover. Capture native insights at 48 hours, roll up Oct 16 and report finally Oct 29.

A directional footfall signal

If the organizer can support it, place a unique creator code word on invitation stories and ask visitors an optional “how did you hear about the mela?” question at entry. Code mentions and answers indicate influence, not exact incrementality. Do not promise direct attribution from views to visits.

07 / Resilience

Plan for the real ground.

The strong plan is the one that survives a cancellation or a wet afternoon.

Creator drops out

Approach a vetted reserve of similar category and tier; @tasty_kaburlu (~104.8K, Vijayawada) is a possible food lead, not a budgeted booking. Confirm format fit, availability and quote. If no in-tier replacement accepts the ceiling, reduce scope or creator count transparently.

Rain at the riverside venue

Build a flexible visit window into contracts. Pivot to covered stalls, hands at work and conversations rather than claiming an outdoor visual regardless of weather.

Quiet weekdays

Use the quieter ground for longer artisan conversations and craft footage. Let local invitation stories target the weekend visit.

Off-brief content

Review reel drafts before posting against the brief: lived experience, women artisans, real voices, craft detail; no scripted government copy, unverified income claims or all-product-shot cuts. Agree one revision round in advance.

08 / In practice

One woman artisan's day.

A sample 45-60 second reel for @hyderabad_weekends (Bhuvi), subject to her participation. Telugu conversation with subtitles where appropriate. Shoot only with the artisan’s consent; never invent a scene or claim.

  1. Open on the stall. Her hands arrange her work as the mela begins.Lived experience, not a generic venue tour.
  2. Move in close. Thread, texture and the maker’s explanation of the finished piece.Craftsmanship in detail.
  3. Listen to her. A short exchange in her own words, with the creator behind the camera.Conversation, not a scripted testimonial.
  4. Meet fellow makers. Show the women working together if they agree to be filmed.Women artisans; Lakhpati Didi context only when verified with the person and organizer, no income figures.
  5. See the ground around her. People browse; a performance is heard or seen if actually happening.Human scale and the mela atmosphere.
  6. Share a pause. A candid meal or a small moment with the artisan, if it occurs naturally.Food and lived experience without staging.
  7. Close with an invitation. The installation at dusk if filmed, then the venue and Oct 10-22 dates.One clear invitation, carried into the second story.
What this avoids

No government-script voiceover, unverified individual income claim, string of product-only shots or statistics replacing a person.
